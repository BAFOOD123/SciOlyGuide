## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Team** | Up to 2 |
| **Time** | About **5 minutes** for **two launches** |
| **Type** | Build (no impound listed) |
| **Eye protection** | **B** (impact-rated Z87+) |
| **You bring** | Up to **two rockets**, **two unaltered standard ping pong balls**, **two parachutes**, and your **flight log**. Optional: your own manual bike pump with a gauge (must fit the provided launcher). |
| **Supervisor provides** | Launcher (Schrader valve), pump, pressure gauge, timers. Indoors with a high ceiling (> 20 ft recommended); **the tournament should tell you the ceiling height in advance**. |
| **Score** | **Longest time aloft** of the parachute payload from a single launch. Tiebreaker: your other launch. |

> [!NOTE]
> This guide summarizes the 2026–27 rules. Check the official manual and the Building Policy on soinc.org.

## How the event runs

1. **Safety inspection** of the rocket(s) and parachutes, and the supervisor reviews your **flight log**.
2. Load the rocket on the launcher (not pressurized), then put the **parachute payload** (ball + parachute) on or in the rocket. After that you can't touch the payload.
3. Pump to your chosen pressure (justified by your log). The supervisor checks the gauge.
4. A team member calls **"3, 2, 1, LAUNCH!"** and releases.
5. **Timing:** from the moment the rocket leaves the launcher until the **ball and parachute land**. Three timers; the middle time counts, to 0.01 s.
6. Make your second launch within the 5 minutes.

## Rules

### Rocket

![Bottle rocket with nose cone and fins](img:ppp-rocket)

- **Pressure vessel:** one plastic **carbonated-beverage bottle of 1 liter or less**, with a nozzle about **2.2 cm** inside diameter and a standard neck (flange to opening under 1.6 cm). **Show the bottle's label.**
- **Don't alter the bottle:** no cutting, sanding, heating, glue (hot glue, super glue) or spray paint on the pressure vessel.
- **Attach everything to the bottle with tape only.** No glue on the pressure vessel.
- **Nothing metal** anywhere on the rocket or payload.
- **Fins and other parts must be at least 5 cm above the bottle's opening** (so it fits the launcher).
- **Nose:** rounded or blunt. When a standard bottle cap (~3.1 cm wide × 1.25 cm tall) is set on the nose, **the nose can't touch the inside top of the cap**. So the tip must be wider than ~3.1 cm or shaped so the cap rests on its sides.
- **Energy only from air pressure** from the launcher's pump. **No water**, other gases, chemicals, explosives, electrical devices, rubber bands, throwing, remote control or tethers.
- **No Mylar or ultrafilm** anywhere.

### Parachute payload

- One **unaltered standard ping pong ball**, attached to the parachute **with tape only**.
- The payload must **separate** from the rocket for full credit.

### Multipliers

| Situation | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| Payload doesn't separate (timing until any part of the rocket touches the ground) | ×0.90 |
| **Incomplete flight log** | ×0.85 (and a capped, supervisor-set pressure) |
| **No flight log** | ×0.70 (and a capped pressure) |
| **Any part hits the ceiling** (or anything attached to it) **on the way up** | **×0.25** (and timing stops at contact) |

Hitting the ceiling is catastrophic. Your pressure choice must get the rocket **close to, but safely below**, the ceiling.

## The flight log

Required: data for **10+ test flights**, with **at least 5 parameters**:

1. **Pressure (psi)**, required.
2. **Estimated peak height (ft)**, required.
3. **Time aloft (s)**, required.
4. and 5. **Two of your own:** e.g., parachute diameter, number of shroud lines, fin count, nose mass, bottle size, deployment method, launch angle.

Use the log to **justify your pressure** for the tournament's ceiling height. A graph of **peak height vs pressure** with your fitted line is the best justification.

| Flight | Pressure (psi) | Est. peak (ft) | Time aloft (s) | Chute diameter (cm) | Shroud lines | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 30 | 14 | 4.8 | 45 | 8 | Chute tangled |
| 2 | 40 | 19 | 7.2 | 45 | 8 | Clean deploy |

**Estimating peak height:** use a clinometer (angle) from a known distance (h = d × tan θ + eye height), or compare against a wall with marked heights, or film it next to a known reference.

## The physics

### Getting height

- Compressed air pushes out of the nozzle, and the rocket accelerates the opposite way (Newton's third law). Air-only rockets get a short, sharp push (much less than water rockets).
- **Higher pressure → more height.** But energy also goes into drag, and heavier rockets coast less.
- Keep the rocket **light**, **stable** and **straight**:
  - **Stability:** the **center of gravity (CG) must be ahead of the center of pressure (CP)**, by at least one body diameter. Add nose mass (no metal: clay or extra tape) or bigger fins at the back to achieve this. Swing test: tie a string at the CG and swing the rocket in a circle. If it flies nose-first, it's stable.
  - **Low drag:** smooth rounded nose, tidy taped fins, nothing flapping.
- Height scales roughly with pressure, so a few psi can mean several feet. Calibrate carefully against your venue's ceiling.

### Staying aloft

![A parachute descending](img:ppp-parachute)

The payload descends at **terminal velocity**, where drag balances weight:

**v_t = √(2mg / (ρ C_d A))**

- m: mass of ball + parachute (a ping pong ball is **2.7 g**). Light parachute materials matter.
- A: parachute area. **Doubling the area cuts descent speed by √2 (~29% slower).**
- C_d: drag coefficient (~0.75–1.5 for round parachutes; a spill hole can steady it).
- ρ: air density (~1.2 kg/m³).

> [!TIP]
> Example: total mass 4 g (0.004 kg), a 50 cm diameter canopy (A = 0.196 m²), C_d = 1.2. v_t = √(2 × 0.004 × 9.81 / (1.2 × 1.2 × 0.196)) ≈ 0.53 m/s. From 6 m (~20 ft), the fall takes about 11 s (plus the ascent time).

**Time aloft = ascent time + descent time.** Key goals:

1. **Get the parachute to open quickly and fully**, right at the top. A chute that opens late loses height.
2. **Big, light canopy:** thin plastic film (trash bag, dry-cleaning bag, tissue paper; not Mylar), many shroud lines of thin thread, the ball centered underneath.
3. **Stable descent:** a small **spill hole** or vent reduces swinging. Lines of equal length.
4. **Avoid tangles:** fold the chute consistently; keep lines from wrapping.

### Separation and deployment

The payload is loaded on top of or in the rocket **after** it's on the launcher, and it has to separate on its own. Common approaches:

- **Payload sits on a cone or cup on the nose**, and simply keeps going (or falls off) when the rocket slows at apogee.
- The ball sits in a **loose cup** at the nose with the chute folded around it, so drag at apogee pulls it free.

Test many times: separation and deployment reliability is what most of your log should be about.

## Tournament strategy

- **Know the ceiling height** (in feet) and choose a pressure that reaches about **80–90% of it** according to your log. A ceiling hit costs 75% of your time.
- Use a safer pressure on the **first launch** to lock in a decent time, then push a little higher on the second if conditions allow.
- Air currents (HVAC, doors) matter. Watch other teams' flights if allowed.
- Bring spare balls, chutes, tape and a backup rocket.

## Common pitfalls

- A pointed nose that touches the inside of the test cap.
- Fins or tape within 5 cm of the bottle opening (it won't fit the launcher).
- Any metal, Mylar or glue on the bottle.
- An incomplete flight log (×0.85): 10 flights and 5 parameters minimum.
- Parachutes tangled in their lines or wrapped around the rocket.

## Test yourself

<details><summary>1. If you double the parachute's area, how does the descent speed change?</summary>

It divides by √2, to about 71% of the original speed.

</details>

<details><summary>2. Why must the center of gravity be in front of the center of pressure?</summary>

So aerodynamic forces turn the nose back into the airflow (weathervane stability) instead of tumbling it.

</details>

<details><summary>3. Your best flight is 9.4 s, but it hit the ceiling on the way up. What's its score?</summary>

Timing stopped at contact and the time is multiplied by 0.25.

</details>

<details><summary>4. What three parameters must the flight log include?</summary>

Pressure (psi), estimated peak height (ft) and time aloft (s), plus at least two more of your choice.

</details>
