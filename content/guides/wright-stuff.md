## The task

Fly a rubber-powered airplane that stays aloft as long as possible in an indoor room. Timing starts when it leaves your hand and stops when it touches the floor (or is caught on a girder, or the supervisor calls it). The **median of three timers** is official. Your best of **2 official flights** counts, multiplied by your flight-log bonus.

## Construction rules

![A rubber-powered indoor airplane](img:wright-airplane)

- **Fits the box** in full **flight configuration** (assembled, ready to fly). After it comes out of the box, nothing about chord, span, length or lifting area may change. Only the **propeller may be rotated** to fit.
- **Airframe mass ≥ 8.0 g** without the motor (bring ballast to adjust for scale differences).
- **Rubber motor(s) ≤ 1.5 g** total (including O-rings). Motors are massed separately and returned at the start of your flight period. Up to 6 may be checked in; they can be lubricated.
- **All lift from the wing(s).** Power only from rubber motor(s) that stay attached.
- **Propellers** may be built or bought, but **no variable-pitch mechanisms** that change diameter or blade angle in flight.
- **Any materials except boron filaments.** Kits must not have pre-glued joints or pre-covered surfaces.
- **No steering** in flight; launch from your hand while standing on the floor (no artificial height aids).

## Scoring

**Flight Score = Flight Time × Flight-Log Bonus.** Your final score is the better of your two official flights.

- **Flight log bonus:** ×1.2 (complete), ×1.1 (partial), ×1.0 (none). A complete log is **free points** — a 120 s flight becomes 144.
- Declare each flight **official or trim** before launch (unspecified = official).

## The flight log (bonus)

A **complete log** has data for **10+ test flights** with **6+ parameters** (3 required + 3 your own):

**Required:** (1) **motor size before wind-up**, (2) **number of turns at launch (or torque at launch)**, (3) **flight time**.

**Additional (pick 3):** turns remaining at landing, peak altitude, torque at landing, propeller pitch, motor width, room/ceiling height, trim settings…

Units are required (torque may be "arbitrary" if your meter is uncalibrated). Use the log to **find your best motor and winding** for the ceiling height.

| Flight | Motor (in × width) | Turns at launch | Time (s) | Turns left | Peak height (ft) | Notes |

| 1 | 16″ × 0.085″ | 1,800 | 95 | 200 | 22 | Climbed too fast, hit ceiling |

## The physics

### Staying up: minimizing sink rate

To maximize time aloft, the plane should fly as **slowly and efficiently** as possible — minimize the **sink rate** (how fast it loses altitude).

- **Light and large wing:** low **wing loading** (weight / wing area) means slow flight and low sink. That's why the minimum mass is only 8 g with a big wing. Use the whole box for span and chord.
- **High lift-to-drag ratio:** a cambered (curved) airfoil, smooth covering (thin Mylar/plastic film over a balsa frame), and clean construction reduce drag.
- **Low Reynolds number flight:** indoor planes fly slowly in thick-feeling air, so thin, highly cambered airfoils and turbulator features work best.
- The plane flies in slow **circles** (to stay in the room and use rising air), so it's trimmed to turn gently.

**Sink rate** ≈ (drag/lift) × airspeed. Minimizing weight and drag and flying near minimum-power speed keeps it airborne longest. A good indoor plane descends at only a few cm/s.

### Power: the rubber motor and propeller

![A wound rubber motor and propeller](img:wright-motor)

- The **rubber motor** stores energy as you wind it; it unwinds to turn the **propeller**, which pulls the plane forward so the wing makes lift.
- **Energy** stored ≈ torque × turns. More and longer rubber stores more energy, but the 1.5 g limit caps it. **Torque** (not just turns) drives the propeller: high torque at launch gives a strong climb; torque falls as it unwinds, giving a long, gentle cruise and descent.
- **Winding:** use a **winder** (a geared crank) and a **torque meter**. Stretch-wind (stretch the motor ~3–4×, wind, then let it back) to pack in more turns safely. Wind close to the motor's break point for max time, with margin.
- **Propeller:** diameter and **pitch** (how far it advances per turn) are tuned to the motor and plane. Larger, higher-pitch props turn slowly and efficiently for long flights. A **freewheeling or folding prop** reduces drag after the motor runs out, extending the glide.

### Hitting the ceiling

Indoor time-aloft is a game of **not hitting the ceiling** while climbing as high as possible. The announced **ceiling height** sets your strategy:

- Wind to a **torque** that climbs the plane to **just below the ceiling**, then cruises and descends slowly.
- Too much torque → it rockets into the ceiling (flight ends). Too little → it never uses the room's height.
- Adjust via turns, motor size, propeller pitch and trim. Your flight log maps turns/torque to peak height for that ceiling.

## Trimming the airplane

**Trim** is adjusting the plane so it flies in smooth, stable circles. Change **one thing at a time** on short test flights:

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| **Dives** | Add up-elevator (tail incidence), move CG back (less nose weight), or reduce wing incidence |
| **Stalls** (nose up, drops, repeats) | Reduce wing incidence or add nose weight (move CG forward) |
| **Won't turn / turns too tight** | Adjust rudder and wing tilt; set a gentle left circle (typical) |
| **Spirals into the ground** | Reduce turn; check for warps |
| **Climbs too steeply then stalls** | Reduce torque/turns or adjust thrust line (down/side thrust) |

- **Center of gravity (CG):** usually around 50–75% of the wing chord for these planes; set by nose/tail ballast.
- **Decalage:** the difference between wing and stabilizer incidence — key to pitch stability.
- **Thrust line:** small down-thrust and side-thrust (right thrust for a left circle) tame the powerful climb.
- Keep wings and tail free of **warps** (they cause unwanted turns and instability).

## Testing and the log

Fly in a space with a similar ceiling. For each test flight, record the required and extra parameters. Build graphs:

- **Turns (or launch torque) vs flight time** for each motor.
- **Peak height vs turns** for the ceiling you'll compete under.
- **Motor cross-section vs performance.**

Use these to pick the motor and winding that climb to just under the ceiling and stay up longest. Carry spare motors, props and repair supplies.

## On competition day

- Check in: the airplane fits the box in flight configuration, airframe ≥ 8 g, motor ≤ 1.5 g.
- Your 10-minute flight period starts when motors are returned. You get **2 official flights** (plus trim flights).
- Declare trim vs official before each launch.
- First official flight: a **safe** winding you know stays below the ceiling. Then push a little on the second.
- Only participants handle the plane; no outside help once in the competition area.

## Common pitfalls

- Airframe under 8 g or motor over 1.5 g.
- The plane not fitting the box in flight configuration.
- Winding too hard and hitting the ceiling (flight ends) or breaking the motor.
- Warped wings causing a dive or spiral.
- No flight log (losing the 20% bonus).
- Forgetting the ceiling-height strategy.
